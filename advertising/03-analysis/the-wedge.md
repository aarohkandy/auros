# THE WEDGE

Who we sell to first, and why it is not who the spec says.

---

## The spec's ordering

Spec §1: *"First customer profile: a nonprofit or small school with 25–200 dead laptops, already living
in Google Workspace or a browser-based system, with one overworked IT person."*

`docs/outreach/PILOT-OFFER.md` ranks the approach list: **(1)** Google-Workspace school or nonprofit,
**(2)** computer-reuse charity or refurbisher, **(3)** public library or community centre.

That ordering is defensible and it was made before three things were known.

## The three things

**1. Schools are the segment where our price is worst.** Microsoft's published education ESU is
$1 / $2 / $4 per device per year. Ours is $15. Over the programme's full run: **$7 versus $45.** We are
six times the price of doing nothing, for a school, until 2028
([the arithmetic](../01-market/the-cost-math-that-inverts.md)).

**2. Refurbishers have a problem with no alternative at all.** Not an expensive alternative — none. A
pallet of 2012–2018 machines cannot take Windows 11 (TPM 2.0, supported CPUs) and the Windows 10
licence they can install expires in 2028. **Stock with no OS path has no resale path**, and that is a
revenue problem, not an IT problem ([refurbishers](../02-cases/adjacent-market/refurbishers.md)).

**3. A refurbisher is a channel; a school is a customer.** One refurbisher touches dozens of schools and
hundreds of households. Landing one is not one sale — it is our image on every machine they ship, via
somebody else's existing sales motion. That is [the Gusto play](../02-cases/yc/gusto.md), and nothing in
our documents identifies the intermediary.

## The reordering

| | Spec / PILOT-OFFER | Proposed | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Google-Workspace school or nonprofit | **Refurbisher / reuse charity** | Has the pile, acute dated problem, days to yes, multiplies |
| 2 | Refurbisher | **Nonprofit at commercial ESU rates** | The one segment where our price wins outright: $45 vs $427 over three years |
| 3 | Library / community centre | **Library / community centre** | Unchanged. Clean kiosk fit, small and discretionary |
| 4 | — | **School** | Best long-term customer, worst first one. Months, committees, cheaper incumbent |

Schools do not leave the plan. They stop being the **first** conversation, because a school is where an
unproven vendor with an empty compatibility table is least likely to be believed and slowest to be told
no.

## Why PILOT-OFFER already has the right instinct

It says of refurbishers: *"they evaluate operating systems for a living — which means they will find our
weaknesses fast. That is a feature at this stage, not a risk."*

That is exactly right and it is an argument for **first**, not second. At Gate 1, with no physical
machine touched, the most valuable thing a first contact can produce is not revenue — it is somebody
competent telling us what is wrong before a school does.

## The qualifying question, adapted

PILOT-OFFER's guard against a pilot ending in a cupboard is: *"what would you do with three more working
computers next week?"*

For a refurbisher the equivalent, and it is sharper because they know the number:

> **"How many machines are sitting in your warehouse right now that you cannot sell because they will
> not take Windows 11?"**

A refurbisher who cannot answer that immediately does not have the problem we think they have, and the
entire wedge is wrong. A refurbisher who answers with a number is describing our market in units we
can count.

## What this does not change

- **No price changes.** §9. The tiers stand exactly as written.
- **No new product.** A refurbisher buys the same thing: a recipe, an image, nightly rebuilds, the
  installer.
- **The honesty envelope.** Every constraint in
  [`../00-constraints/`](../00-constraints/) applies identically.
- **Spec §11.** Zero-terminal and Windows-shaped matter *more* here, because the refurbisher eats every
  support call the machine generates after resale. It is their commercial risk, which makes it a
  purchasing criterion rather than a nicety.

## The honest weakness of this entire argument

**We have not spoken to a single refurbisher.** The wedge is inferred from published licensing terms and
published hardware requirements. It is not inferred from anybody in the business saying *yes, that is
my problem.*

It could be wrong three ways: they already install Linux on unsellable stock; they are exiting old
hardware rather than trying to sell it; or MAR programme terms complicate shipping a non-Microsoft OS
on machines in the programme.

**All three are settled by one twenty-minute conversation**, which needs a human (§9, real inbox) and
is the highest-value twenty minutes available to this company. It is item one in
[`../04-plan/30-60-90.md`](../04-plan/30-60-90.md), and the plan is explicitly written to survive the
answer being no — because the compatibility table, the honest positioning and the pilot mechanics are
worth building for any of the four segments.
