## Why the 2028 wall is the real story and the 2026 date is only the noise

A machine from 2012–2018 mostly **cannot run Windows 11** — the TPM 2.0 and supported-CPU requirements
are the reason these machines are in the cupboard rather than upgraded. So ESU is not a bridge to
anywhere. It is a **metered countdown on a machine with no destination**, and the meter doubles every
year (see [the cost arithmetic](the-cost-math-that-inverts.md)).

That reframes what we are selling. We are not competing with Windows 10. We are competing with a
**decision that has been deferred three times and cannot be deferred a fourth**.

## How big the deferred pile is

- HP and Dell both estimated **roughly half of all PCs** were still on Windows 10 at the October 2025
  cutoff ([Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/microsoft/windows-10/windows-10-eol-esu-faq)).
- The End of 10 campaign frames the reuse opportunity at **~400 million computers**
  ([endof10.org](https://endof10.org/)).
- Desktop Linux share is reported at **4.7%**, the highest recorded
  ([Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/end-of-10-campaign-highlights-benefits-of-linux-transition/)).

**What we may do with these numbers:** cite them, with the source and the date, as somebody else's
measurement about the market. **What we may not do:** convert any of them into a claim about machines
*we* have migrated, money *we* have saved, or organisations *we* serve. The honesty gate's
`device-count` and `savings-figure` rules will catch the attempt, which is the point of having them.

## The two media waves, and which one is ours

**Wave 1 — 2025-10-14.** Already happened. Peak coverage, peak "should I switch to Linux" searching.
We missed it because we did not exist.

**Wave 2 — 2026-10-13.** Twenty-three days away. Smaller than wave 1 and differently shaped: the story
is no longer "a deadline is coming", it is **"the deadline came and 
a lot of machines are still here."** That is a better story for us, because it is about the machines
rather than about the announcement.

**We cannot ship a product into wave 2.** Gate 1 is not closed, no image has published, no laptop has
been touched. Anyone planning a launch around 2026-10-13 is planning a launch of nothing.

**We can ship a *measurement* into wave 2**, and that is what
[`04-plan/STRATEGY.md`](../04-plan/STRATEGY.md) proposes: not an announcement, a dataset with our own
failures in it. The wave is not our launch. It is the first time our compatibility table gets read.

## The date that actually governs our funnel

Not 2026-10-13. It is whenever a given organisation's **budget cycle** forces the decision, which for
schools is usually the spring before a September. That means the sale we can realistically close is
for **academic year 2027–28**, and the work between now and then is building the thing that is already
ranking and already trusted when that conversation starts.

An advertising plan that treats 2026-10-13 as a launch date is wrong by about a year. An advertising
plan that ignores it wastes the only free attention this market will get.
