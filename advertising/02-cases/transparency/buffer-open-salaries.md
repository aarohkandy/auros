# BUFFER — open salaries and public metrics

**Steal: 5 · Fit: 4 · Here mostly as a boundary marker.**

---

## The play

Buffer published employee salaries, the compensation formula, revenue, and its fundraising terms. It
generated substantial press and became a hiring advantage in a crowded market.

## Why it scores low for us

- **The audience was talent and press**, not buyers. Neither is our constraint.
- **It needs a company with employees and revenue** to be transparent about. We have neither.
- **The press value was novelty.** It is no longer novel, and repeating it now reads as imitation.

## The one thing worth extracting

Buffer's transparency was about **the company**. Backblaze's, Cloudflare's and GitLab's is about **the
work**.

Transparency about the work produces an artifact the buyer uses — a dataset, a post-mortem, a handbook.
Transparency about the company produces an article somebody reads once.

**We should do the second kind and skip the first.** Our salary is not interesting and our revenue is
zero. What is interesting is that we found sixteen shell-injection sites in our own workflows and wrote
down each one.

## The boundary this marks

There is a version of "be transparent" that is performative and buys nothing. The distinguishing
question is: **does the reader get something they can use?** A compatibility row, a post-mortem, a
decision log with the reasoning — yes. A salary table from a company with no customers — no.
