# ANTI-PATTERN: "THE YEAR OF THE LINUX DESKTOP"

**Twenty-plus years of advocacy marketing that never converted an institution.**

---

## The pattern

Desktop Linux advocacy has, for decades, sold the OS on its properties: free, open, secure, fast,
private, not-Microsoft. The message is aimed at people who already care about operating systems.

Share is now reported at **4.7%**, the highest ever recorded
([Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/end-of-10-campaign-highlights-benefits-of-linux-transition/)) — a
genuine milestone, and it took twenty years.

## Why it fails with institutions specifically

A school does not have an opinion about operating systems and should not be asked to acquire one.
Nobody in the building wants Linux. They want:

- machines that work
- that nobody has to defend to a governor or an auditor
- that do not generate support calls
- and that do not need a budget line

**Every advocacy argument answers a question they did not ask.** "It's free and open source" is heard
as "it is unsupported and you are on your own", which for an organisation with one overworked IT person
is a reasonable inference.

## What the spec already gets right

Spec §11 (D4, the human's directive) is an explicit rejection of this pattern: *"make it feel like
personalized Windows rather than linux rn which needs you to memorize books of commands."*

Zero-terminal. Windows-shaped by default. Clean end to end. **None of that is about Linux**, and the
word barely appears in the product directive.

And the landing page opens on the machine, not the OS: *"A 2014 laptop opens a spreadsheet as fast as
it did in 2014. Nothing about it wore out."*

## The rule for every piece of copy

**Never sell the operating system. Sell the absence of a problem they already have.**

| Do not say | Say |
|---|---|
| "Linux is secure" | "An unpatched machine on a school network is a decision somebody has to defend" |
| "Free and open source" | "The machines you already own, still getting patched in four years" |
| "No bloatware" | "Eleven applications. The build reports what it removed, by name, with the size" |
| "Take back control" | "One conversation about what the machines are for. That conversation is the recipe" |

## The nuance, because the advocacy audience is not worthless

[The End of 10 campaign](../adjacent-market/end-of-10.md) is advocacy marketing and it is **working** —
because it is aimed at individuals, who *can* be persuaded by values, and because it attaches a local
volunteer to the message.

So the rule is not "advocacy never works". It is: **advocacy converts individuals, never institutions.**
Our product is institutional. The one place advocacy helps us is with the volunteer and the
refurbisher's technical lead — people who are already persuaded and who have institutions in their
address book.
