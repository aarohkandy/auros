# THE END OF 10 CAMPAIGN

**Steal: 8 · Fit: 9 · The nearest thing to a free channel, and it carries a real risk.**

---

## What it is

A coalition campaign — backed by **Debian, Fedora, GNOME, KDE and Zorin** — with one message:
*"Don't buy a new PC — revive yours with Linux."* It connects people to **local volunteer groups and
repair cafés** who will do the install in person
([endof10.org](https://endof10.org/),
[Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/end-of-10-campaign-highlights-benefits-of-linux-transition/)).

The Restart Project publishes a toolkit for community repair groups
([therestartproject.org](https://therestartproject.org/end-of-windows-10-toolkit-for-repair-groups/)),
and Linux-specific repair cafés were trialled in Amsterdam by Repair Café International.

They frame the opportunity at **~400 million computers**, and desktop Linux share is reported at
**4.7%**, the highest recorded.

## Why it is worth studying regardless of whether we ever touch it

1. **They solved the message.** *"Your computer still works. Microsoft says it doesn't count any more.
   Here is a free way to make it count again."* That is better than anything on our landing page: it
   starts from the object in the room, blames nobody personally, and makes the next step small. Compare
   our *"The machines are not broken. They are orphaned."* — the same idea, one notch more abstract.
2. **They proved the demand exists without any product marketing at all.** Pure message, no vendor.
3. **The local-volunteer directory is the distribution insight.** The campaign's real product is a map
   from *"I have this problem"* to *"here is a human near you"*. Trust in this market is local.

## The gap they leave, which is exactly our shape

A repair café will install Linux on your laptop on a Saturday. **Nobody in that network will keep 40
machines patched for four years with no one on site.** The campaign converts individuals; it has no
answer for an organisation. Spec §1.1 is the sentence that fits into that gap: *"The OS is not the
product. The maintained image is the product."*

## The risk, stated plainly

**This is a free-software coalition and D30 made us all rights reserved**, while building on Fedora and
Aurora — two of the projects behind the campaign. Turning up as a proprietary vendor to ask for a
listing, without volunteering that, is how a company acquires a thread it does not recover from.

The honest version is defensible and possibly strong:
- Our tooling is ours; we say so in `LICENSING.md` rather than burying it.
- The **image contains GPL and LGPL software and recipients hold those rights directly** — we cannot
  withhold them and we do not claim to.
- We would contribute the compatibility data publicly, which is a real contribution back to the exact
  problem the campaign exists to solve.

That is a real position, honestly stated. It may still get a hostile reception, and going in expecting
otherwise is the mistake. See [`../../03-analysis/what-would-make-this-fail.md`](../../03-analysis/what-would-make-this-fail.md).
