# ZORIN OS

**Steal: 7 · Fit: 9 · Closest positioning to ours; instructive on both sides.**

---

## What they are

Windows-shaped Linux for people leaving Windows. A dedicated [education page](https://zorin.com/os/education/),
a paid Pro tier, and a backer of the End of 10 campaign.

Their desktop pitch and spec §11's product directive are almost the same sentence: *make it feel like
personalized Windows rather than linux rn which needs you to memorize books of commands.*

## What they get right, worth copying

- **They sell the feeling, not the kernel.** Nothing on the front page is about Linux as a technology.
- **The education page exists and is specific.** They understood early that a school is a different
  buyer from an enthusiast.
- **A paid tier on a free OS, without shame.** Useful precedent: people do pay for a desktop Linux
  product when the pitch is time saved rather than software received.
- **Coalition membership.** Being one of the named backers of End of 10 buys distribution no
  advertising budget would.

## Where we differ, and why the difference is the whole business

Zorin ships **one OS to everybody**. We ship **one image per organisation**, built from a recipe, with
a removal report naming what was taken out.

That difference is not a feature comparison, it is a different product category: they sell a
distribution, we sell a maintained image and the fleet behind it. Their customer installs; ours is
enrolled.

## The cautionary half

**Zorin Grid — their fleet-management product — was announced in January 2020**
([Zorin blog](https://blog.zorin.com/2020/01/16/introducing-zorin-grid-manage-all-of-your-organizations-computers-as-easily-as-one/),
[Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jasonevangelho/2020/01/16/meet-zorin-grid-a-slick-solution-for-businesses-ditching-windows-7-for-linux/))
— and per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zorin_OS) **remains unreleased as of April 2026.**

Six years. Full analysis in [`../anti-patterns/zorin-grid.md`](../anti-patterns/zorin-grid.md).

**The thing to take from it:** the management layer is the hardest part of this business, not the
desktop. The company nearest to us in positioning has been unable to ship it for six years while
running a real product with real users. Spec §6E — *"Build last, only after a real customer exists. Do
not build this speculatively"* — is not caution. It is the correct read of a market where someone else
has already spent six years proving the point.
